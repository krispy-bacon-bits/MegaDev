Counted the way the desk counts: one id, one report, each under the cause its tenant puts it in. The export holds 427 rows for #4471, and 15 of them repeat an id from an overlapping page. 5 of the one-day move reports are also counted under parser, where the desk files them.

The problems

parser (issue 1, fixed). The shared parser read every date month first, and every tenant on #4471 writes the day first. Day and month came out swapped. “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” Engineering fixed it in fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales.

hint (issue 2, won’t fix). The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date day first. People type what the hint tells them and the date comes out swapped. This form already reads day first, so the parser fix does not change it. Engineering retired the booking-v3 form, so issue 2 is closed as not planned.

one-day move (issue 3, open). These dates are one day off, not swapped. “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” That points at time zone handling. The desk listed these reports in desk-notes.json, but the table on #4471 still counts them as parser.

The one-day moves were never part of the parser fix, and the engineering update names no fix for them. Issue 3 stays open until one lands. These reports no longer have an open desk ticket, so this is the only place they are tracked.

How the reports were counted

  • One id, one report. The export pages overlap, so some reports appear twice.
  • A report goes where its tenant’s locale, booking form and channel put it (causes.ts), not where intake tagged it. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser, and the tags above show how often it guessed wrong.
  • The one-day moves come from the desk’s own reading in desk-notes.json. They stay counted where the desk files them and get a row of their own. When the desk adds a report there, it joins that row on the next build.
  • Matching on subject line (decision 0001) is how all of this ended up in one ticket. The same subject line turns up under different problems, so subjects cannot tell them apart.
